# Editable scene assets

The spacecraft is built from editable TypeScript geometry in `components/spacecraft-model.ts`; no purchased model, Blender installation, or mockup bitmap is required. Cream pressure frames, raised graphite seals, amber handles, interior lighting, paired solar wings and furnishings follow the supplied toybox references. Repeated parts are instanced or batched by material. About has a closed aft bulkhead and the docking rings are separated surfaces rather than intersecting, tangent rings.

The cabins form a 2×2 grid facing +Z: Projects (−1.65,+1.70), Experience (+1.65,+1.70), About (−1.65,−1.70), Contact (+1.65,−1.70). The original docking sleeve and dish remain above left; the service module and paired solar wings sit on the right. The Contact cabin adds a radio display, speaker, amber receiver and controls.

`createSpacecraft(THREE, {accent, labels, projects, sampleLabel, projectPageSize, screenLabels})` returns the scene group, picking targets, live `readerSurfaces`, project setters and `update`. The update accepts active room, selected/hovered project, page, reading state and elapsed delta. `group.userData` provides room bounds, room/reader/label anchors, hotspots and measured overview bounds. The renderer consumes this contract instead of duplicating cabin coordinates. `PROJECTS_PER_PAGE` in `lib/flight.ts` is nine: arbitrary project collections page through a 3×3 rack, with empty positions disabled. All visible owner labels and project titles come from the database.

Principal room labels are screen-projected native buttons. They stay upright as the ship tilts, resize with projected room width, and highlight on pointer or keyboard focus. Model-only consumers can retain baked nameplates by leaving `screenLabels` false.

Projects open hinged lockers and a clipboard; experience uses a mission console; About opens a bound journal; contact uses a communications instrument. `components/world-reader.tsx` renders actual semantic HTML onto the corresponding Three.js attachment with `CSS3DRenderer`. Each frame copies the physical surface's world transform, including partial deployment. Portrait screens stretch the physical housing while retaining normal-sized HTML text. The document itself scrolls by touch, wheel and keyboard. Its return and page controls remain fixed within the instrument.

`components/spacecraft.tsx` owns fixed camera flights, lighting, input, picking and disposal. Pointer movement adds bounded camera rotation (at most ±0.045 radians yaw and ±0.025 pitch); it does not accumulate during dragging. Reading freezes this response. Idle interiors dim to 73% diffuse color and 0.48 point-light intensity. Hover restores color and raises the room light to 2.85; selection uses normal 2.1 lighting. Individual hovered project drawers extend 0.075 world units and gain a perimeter light. Overview hover adds amber perimeter/floor guides, a small target shift and a 2.5% approach. Phones turn the vessel into a portrait composition and fit its bounds; nameplates reposition below their projected cabin centers in the viewer’s orientation. Position, distance, rotation, pointer response and hover approach retain velocity when targets change; substepped springs cap acceleration and speed. Pause freezes their current values. Browser history and navigation preserve the renderer.

Lighting uses ACES tone mapping, image-based fill, warm key/rim lighting and cached PCF shadows. Capable desktop renderers add denoised GTAO contact shading, recalculated when the camera or physical objects move. Moving doors and readers do not invalidate the static shadow map. A single requestAnimationFrame loop avoids the irregular cadence of the previous frame limiter. Pixel ratio is capped at 2 desktop and 1.75 mobile. Pause, reduced motion, hidden/offscreen states and reading mode stop unnecessary animation; simulation advances only with active time. Picking uses simple volumes and thirteen object targets rather than tracing detailed geometry.

`components/orbital-environment.ts` renders a procedural ocean planet, generated white clouds, a blue atmospheric limb, a navy star field, restrained twinkle and two staggered meteor streams. No Earth image assets or image requests remain. Cloud fields consume about 0.417MiB of texture payload on desktop or0.073MiB mobile; see [EARTH-ASSETS.md](EARTH-ASSETS.md). All effects share the active clock. Generation is synchronous and takes milliseconds; a themed loader covers scene initialization until its first rendered frame.

`components/immersive-portfolio.tsx` maintains meaningful URLs and server-consistent metadata. Full content is server-rendered before enhancement. Reading view removes GPU work; Save-Data and WebGL loss fall back to HTML. Very short viewports and browser magnification use reading view, while a focused contact field suppresses disruptive keyboard-resize switching. Drafts and submission state live above both readers and survive switching. They are held in memory, not local storage.

Geometry, materials, textures, framebuffers, shadows, observers and listeners are disposed on exit. The scene and native instrument integration add initial engineering and GPU cost compared with a static page. Procedural source and database-driven labels keep ongoing content work independent of 3D tooling. Small generated fields replace image resolution tiers; desktop contact shading is still limited to capable renderers. Physical-device and assistive-technology testing remain useful before a public launch.
