# Editable scene assets

The spacecraft is built from editable TypeScript geometry in `components/spacecraft-model.ts`; no purchased model, Blender installation, or mockup bitmap is required. Cream pressure frames, raised graphite seals, amber handles, interior lighting, paired solar wings and furnishings follow the supplied toybox references. Repeated parts are instanced or batched by material. About has a closed aft bulkhead and the docking rings are separated surfaces rather than intersecting, tangent rings.

The centerline runs along X; +Y is up and +Z faces the open reading side. Projects, experience and About sit at X −3, 0 and 3; communications at X −5.5. `createSpacecraft(THREE, {accent, labels, projects, sampleLabel})` returns the scene group, picking targets, `readerSurfaces`, `setProjectPage`, `setProjects`, `setReading`, and `update`. The update accepts active room, selected project, page, reading state and elapsed delta. Labels, titles, sample markings and accent color come from database content. Arbitrarily many projects page through the three physical lockers; adding records never requires editing the model.

Projects open hinged lockers and a clipboard; experience uses a mission console; About opens a bound journal; contact uses a communications instrument. `components/world-reader.tsx` renders actual semantic HTML onto the corresponding Three.js attachment with `CSS3DRenderer`. Each frame copies the physical surface's world transform, including partial deployment. Portrait screens stretch the physical housing while retaining normal-sized HTML text. The document itself scrolls by touch, wheel and keyboard. Its return and page controls remain fixed within the instrument.

`components/spacecraft.tsx` owns fixed camera flights, lighting, input, picking and disposal. Pointer movement adds bounded camera rotation (at most ±0.045 radians yaw and ±0.025 pitch); it does not accumulate during dragging. Reading freezes this response. Overview hover adds amber perimeter/floor guides, a small target shift and a 2.5% approach. Phones use a fixed diagonal overview composition. Browser history and navigation preserve the renderer.

Lighting uses ACES tone mapping, image-based fill, warm key/rim lighting and cached PCF shadows. Capable desktop renderers add denoised GTAO contact shading, recalculated when the camera or physical objects move. Moving doors and readers do not invalidate the static shadow map. A single requestAnimationFrame loop avoids the irregular cadence of the previous frame limiter. Pixel ratio is capped at 2 desktop and 1.75 mobile. Pause, reduced motion, hidden/offscreen states and reading mode stop unnecessary animation; simulation advances only with active time. Picking uses simple volumes and seven object targets rather than tracing detailed geometry.

`components/orbital-environment.ts` renders a procedural navy star field, restrained twinkle, an occasional shooting star, NASA Earth/cloud maps and an atmospheric limb. Earth rotates at 0.0015 radians/second, clouds at 0.0021. All motion shares the pause clock. Map tiers are described in [EARTH-ASSETS.md](EARTH-ASSETS.md). The environment remains hidden until both Earth maps are ready; loading failure leaves the ship and stars usable. All runtime assets are local.

`components/immersive-portfolio.tsx` maintains meaningful URLs and server-consistent metadata. Full content is server-rendered before enhancement. Reading view removes GPU work; Save-Data and WebGL loss fall back to HTML. Very short viewports and browser magnification use reading view, while a focused contact field suppresses disruptive keyboard-resize switching. Drafts and submission state live above both readers and survive switching. They are held in memory, not local storage.

Geometry, materials, textures, framebuffers, shadows, observers and listeners are disposed on exit. The scene and native instrument integration add initial engineering and GPU cost compared with a static page. Procedural source and database-driven labels keep ongoing content work independent of 3D tooling. Unknown-memory devices use 4K Earth maps; only explicitly capable devices can select the larger 8K cloud texture. Physical-device and assistive-technology testing remain useful before a public launch.
