# Engineering service spine: independent visual review

**Final scoped score: 95/100. Pass.**

Reviewed the approved whole-ship and closeup Version 2 concepts against the final desktop overview, final portrait overview, revised bay detail, and final Projects-room oblique screenshot. This is a visual review of the requested static ladder bay. It does not score unrelated rooms, new interactions, navigation, or camera changes. The existing curved lining and clear zero-gravity passage are intentional constraints; a new floor platform is not required.

## Findings

- **Ladder construction and attachment — 24/25.** The revised satin silver treads read clearly between orange rails. Dark collars and visible support brackets make the connections credible. The oblique view confirms visible stand-offs, plate fasteners, and separation from the backing. Fitted upper and lower attachments finish both ends without requiring a new platform.
- **Conduit integration — 23/25.** Upper and lower bundles remain contained inside the fitted recesses in the reviewed views. Brighter alloy risers and protected end fixtures distinguish the hardware from the dark wells. The conduit detail is visually quieter and less materially rich than the concept, but coherent with the existing scene.
- **Backing and bay depth — 19/20.** Segmented graphite panels provide the intended engineering character. Panel seams, fastening points, and the oblique depth view prevent the assembly from reading as a flat graphic.
- **Grilles and guarded controls — 15/15.** The compact grille pairs and three inset guarded switches fit the available service strip and preserve readable spacing. No visibly escaping or overlapping elements appear in the provided views.
- **Material, lighting, and ship cohesion — 14/15.** Orange, cream, graphite, and alloy match the spacecraft palette. The final illuminated header integrates the ladder with the ship's utility lighting. The bay remains recognizable in the portrait overview at reduced scale.

## Conclusion

The revisions resolve the first candidate's substantive visual weaknesses: dark rung treads, weak end termination, and poorly legible service hardware. No remaining visual issue warrants another implementation iteration within the requested scope. The remaining difference from the AI concept is chiefly surface and lighting richness, not a visible construction or integration failure.

The numerical geometry checks reported by the implementation team were not independently rerun in this visual review. This sign-off is based on the supplied rendered evidence.
