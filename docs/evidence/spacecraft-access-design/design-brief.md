# Spacecraft access equipment — design brief

15 September 2026. Baseline `234de17`.

The user rejected the previous ladder-end and exterior covers as generic filler.
They explicitly prioritize design over optimization and suggested exterior ladders.
The new work therefore spends geometry on recognizable physical functions rather
than retaining the previous panel footprints or triangle limits.

The exterior concept is an open EVA access route from the docking shoulder across
the roof: raised paired rails, spaced rungs, visible stand-offs and restraint eyes.
The lower docking shoulder receives a short transfer station. Interior ladder ends
use rigid grab bars with rounded returns and recognizable tether hardware. The important
visual test is whether their purpose is apparent at the supported camera angles,
without adding labels to explain unfamiliar boxes.

The shared palette uses graphite structure, satin-alloy joints and touch surfaces,
cream mounting surfaces where appropriate, and restrained amber identification.
The existing door handles, ladder and docking ring establish the color context.
Some clear hull remains between useful fittings; coverage alone is not a design
success criterion.

NASA's discussion of mobility aids supports the underlying functions: handholds
and restraints help occupants move, stop and change direction in microgravity,
and consistent visual cues make such fittings identifiable. This is inspiration
for the art direction, not a claim of tested spacecraft engineering or compliance.
[NASA, Architecture §8.5](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/8-0-architecture-vol-2/).

The independent critic's rubric now weights recognizable function (30),
composition/material harmony (25), physical attachment/clearance (20), request
fulfillment (15), code/cost transparency (5) and regression evidence (5). The
previous technically sound fit and inexpensive geometry did not make the covers
a successful design for this user. Visual acceptance must come before accounting
for the new structural cost. Held optimization candidates remain held.

## First integrated visual review

The first exterior candidate passed the visual direction check: its open rungs,
raised rails and mounting feet communicate an access route. In the shallow
overview it reads primarily as a handrail; the supported roof angles expose the
rungs. It uses the nearby dark structure and alloy finishes without excessive
orange.

The first interior candidate was rejected during independent review. Its
sinuous black transfer grips resembled hoses, and the thin tether drum read as a
metal disk. The requested revision uses rigid grab-bar silhouettes, identifiable
grasp sections, a deeper winding drum and a clearer retained hook. The review
also expanded to the older framed cassettes at the docking shoulder: leaving
those would preserve the same generic-box vocabulary. Their replacement is a
matched pair of holstered rescue lights, one at each deck.
